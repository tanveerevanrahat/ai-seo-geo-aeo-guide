# AI SEO, GEO & AEO: How to Rank in ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews

Search has changed. People no longer only click ten blue links — they ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Google AI Overviews, and read the answer right there. If your business is not cited in that answer, you are invisible to those users, even if you rank on page one.

This guide explains the shift in plain language and gives you a practical framework.

## Why classic SEO alone is not enough

Classic SEO optimizes pages to rank. AI search optimizes entities to be cited. An AI answer picks sources it trusts: clear entities, direct answers, structured facts, and real-world proof. A page can rank and still never be cited if the AI cannot understand what the business is, or cannot trust it.

| Classic SEO | GEO & AEO |
|---|---|
| Goal: rank a page | Goal: get cited in an AI answer |
| Keyword-focused | Entity- and question-focused |
| Clicks come from rankings | Visibility comes from citations |
| Success = position | Success = mentions + citations |

## The 7-step framework

1. **Entity clarity.** State clearly who you are, what you do, and where. One business, one consistent name, address, and description everywhere.
2. **Direct answers first.** Every important page should answer its core question in the first 40–80 words, then explain.
3. **Question-based structure.** Use real questions as headings (what, why, how, which, cost, where) and answer each one fully.
4. **Structured data.** Use schema markup so machines can read your facts: organization, services, FAQs, and reviews.
5. **Proof, not claims.** Show real results with real numbers. For example, our work for RFL Eshop grew monthly visitors from 389 to 96,600+ (1.2M+ impressions, documented in Search Console).
6. **Freshness.** Update key pages regularly. AI systems prefer current, maintained sources.
7. **Brand mentions.** Get your brand mentioned (with or without a link) on relevant sites, profiles, and articles. Mentions build entity authority.

## Common mistakes

- Writing for keywords instead of questions
- Hiding the answer under long introductions
- Inconsistent business details across the web
- Claiming results you cannot prove
- Publishing thin pages at scale and hoping AI will cite them
